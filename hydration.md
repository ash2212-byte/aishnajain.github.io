# A Study of Bio-Impedance with Human Skin Hydration variations

## Overview

Can electrical impedance be used as a non-invasive indicator of skin hydration?

This project investigated the relationship between skin hydration and bio-impedance using frequency-based electrical measurements. Bio-Impedance Analysis (BIA) provides a potentially low-cost and non-invasive approach to assessing hydration, but applying it specifically to skin presents challenges related to electrode design, frequency selection, material properties, and measurement sensitivity.

The objective was to experimentally correlate skin hydration with bio-impedance across different frequencies and investigate whether changes in hydration could be reliably detected through impedance measurements.

---

## My role

- Studying the principles and applications of Bio-Impedance Analysis (BIA) for hydration assessment.
- Reviewing existing approaches including single-frequency BIA, multi-frequency BIA, Bio-Impedance Spectroscopy, and Bio-Impedance Vector Analysis.
- Designing an experimental approach to compare dry and hydrated skin conditions.
- Investigating the fabrication of flexible interdigitated electrodes (IDEs).
- Evaluating thin-film electrode fabrication using sputtering and conductive-ink-based approaches.
- Developing the experimental setup using an LCR meter, IDEs, and controlled hydration conditions.
- Analysing impedance variation as a function of frequency.
- Identifying experimental errors and limitations in electrode design and measurement methodology.

## Results

Methodology-I: Thin-Film Flexible Electrodes: Sputtering

The first fabrication approach encountered significant practical challenges.

Non-uniform sputtering resulted in an open circuit, preventing reliable current flow through the electrode structure. Recoating using epoxy and silver paste was attempted, but the circuit still failed to produce the required electrical response.

This led to the decision to transition to the second electrode methodology.

Rather than treating fabrication failure simply as an experimental setback, it revealed an important design constraint:
The electrode itself must be engineered to provide sufficient electrical resistance relative to the skin test section.

Methodology-II: Copper Foil Electrodes

Copper foils were introduced as an alternative electrode configuration to obtain measurable electrical responses.
The impedance-frequency behaviour was then compared between the different hydration conditions.

The expected physical trend was that increased hydration should reduce skin resistance and therefore reduce impedance. However, the measured response did not consistently follow this expected trend, particularly at lower frequencies.
This discrepancy became an important part of the analysis rather than being treated as a successful/unsuccessful binary outcome.

While the experimental results did not consistently reproduce the expected hydration-dependent impedance trend, the investigation successfully identified key limitations in the electrode configuration and established design requirements for a more sensitive future measurement system.

The next step would be to fabricate a thin, high-resistance IDE using controlled deposition or printing and repeat the multi-frequency measurements under standardized environmental and skin conditions.

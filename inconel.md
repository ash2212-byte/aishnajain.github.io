# Machining of Inconel 617

### Experimental Investigation of Tool Performance, Surface Roughness & Metallurgical Characteristics

**Machining · Nickel-Based Superalloys · Tool Wear · Surface Roughness · SEM/EDS · Hardness Testing**

## Overview

**Inconel 617** is a nickel-based superalloy designed for demanding high-temperature applications. Its high-temperature strength, oxidation and corrosion resistance, low thermal conductivity, and rapid strain hardening make it attractive for applications such as **gas turbine components, combustion chambers, heat exchangers, and chemical-processing equipment**. These same properties, however, make Inconel difficult to machine.
This project investigated the **machinability of Inconel 617**, focusing on cutting-tool behaviour, surface roughness, machining challenges, and the metallurgical characteristics of the machined material.

The study combined **machining experiments with materials characterization** to understand how tool selection and cutting conditions influence machining performance.

## My Role
- Studying the machining behaviour of nickel-based superalloys.
- Analysing the influence of cutting speed and tool selection on machining performance.
-  Measuring surface roughness after machining.
-  Analysing the experimental results and identifying abnormal tool behaviour.
-  Participating in metallurgical characterization through microstructure, composition, EDS, mapping, and hardness analysis.

The machining experiments were structured around different cutting speeds while maintaining:

**Depth of cut:** 1 mm
**Feed:** 0.2 mm/rev

Three principal cutting-speed conditions were investigated:

### Set 1 — 100 m/min

Tools:

* Uncoated SiAlON
* Uncoated whisker-reinforced aluminium
* AlTiN-coated ceramic

### Set 2 — 200 m/min

Tools:

* Uncoated SiAlON
* Uncoated whisker-reinforced aluminium
* AlTiN-coated ceramic

### Set 3 — 300 m/min

Tools:

* Uncoated SiAlON
* Uncoated whisker-reinforced aluminium
* AlTiN-coated ceramic

## Measurement & Characterization

The experimental setup incorporated multiple measurement techniques to capture both machining performance and material response.

### Dynamometer

Used as part of the machining setup to investigate cutting behaviour and forces.

### Surface Roughness Measurement

A portable surface roughness tester was used to quantify the resulting machined surface.

### Infrared Camera

Used to observe thermal behaviour associated with machining.

---

## Results

Surface roughness measurements demonstrated differences between tool configurations.


An important experimental observation was the abnormal behaviour of the coated tungsten-carbide tool. Rather than simply continuing with the original test matrix, the experimental plan was adapted and trials were subsequently conducted using uncoated SiAlON under another set of parameters.

This iterative approach was an important part of the experimental investigation.


The project extended beyond machining performance into **microstructural and compositional characterization**.

The project incorporated:

**Optical / Microscopic Examination**
Investigation of material microstructure.

**EDS**
Elemental composition analysis.

**Elemental Mapping**
Spatial distribution of elements within the material.

**Vickers Hardness Testing**
Assessment of the material's hardness following the experimental investigation.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Key Engineering Insight

The project demonstrated that machining a nickel-based superalloy requires consideration of the **entire machining system**:

**Workpiece Properties → Tool Material → Cutting Parameters → Temperature → Tool Wear → Surface Integrity**

A tool that performs well based on one metric may not necessarily provide the best overall machining performance.

This makes **experimental comparison and characterization** essential when selecting tools for difficult-to-machine superalloys.
